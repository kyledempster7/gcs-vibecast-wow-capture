## Talking bullets (5–8)

1. **Tone shock (public high-level).** After Icecrown’s gothic finality, MoP *looks* soft — new continent, Pandaren, martial-arts fantasy, discovery energy. The “cute” first impression is real and fair.

2. **What the plot actually does (public high-level, no quest dump).** Horde and Alliance crash into Pandaria chasing war; they awaken old powers and **faction leadership cracks** under the weight. Sha = “negative emotion made monster” as a readable metaphor. Garrosh’s arc turns the expansion from travelogue into civil war.

3. **Wrath contrast.** Wrath: clear monster-king, you are the coalition hero. MoP: the continent is not the only villain — **we** import the war. That is the feeling to name out loud for Mac.

4. **Systems that stuck (public high-level).**  
   - **Monk** as a permanent class fantasy.  
   - **Scenarios** / flexible small-group story chunks (shape of “short content”).  
   - **Farm / daily hub culture** (Tillers-type life sim energy — name the vibe, don’t list dailies).  
   - **Challenge modes / tight dungeon timer culture** as a design ancestor people still reference when they talk modern M+.  
   - **Timeless Isle–style open sandbox** end-of-exp vibe (optional mention).

5. **What a cold Wrath vet misses first.** Not every rare or rep. The **emotional turn**: discovery → “why are we still fighting here?” → Orgrimmar-as-raid climax energy. If you only remember “pandas,” you missed the second half.

6. **Character bridge (vault).** We don’t have a dedicated MoP campaign log in vault. Live identity scars are heavier in **Legion** (Pain Deathlord, Havoc DH) and **Wrath zones we still walk** (Agony + Mac Howling Fjord return). Optional class-flavor only: Agony’s **green fire** interest lives in spooky taxonomy (not expansion story SoT). MoP campaign is the **shared blank** — perfect honest track: “we weren’t here for this chapter.”

7. **Duo angle.** Good co-op *feel* expansion: beautiful open zones, story beats you can react to together without needing mythic context. Chromie-time MoP later is optional play, not required for this audio track.

8. **One sentence takeaway.** MoP is where modern WoW learned that a “funny continent” can still deliver a **political tragedy** — and that systems experiments (monks, scenarios, farm life) outlive the patch cycle.

## Bridge to Midnight

Pandaria is the first big post-Wrath lesson for a returning brain: **the map can feel alien and the war can still be about us.** Later expansions keep that pattern — new texture, same faction gravity — until the story pivots from “who rules Orgrimmar?” to “what is wrong with the world-soul?” Midnight-era talk sits at the far end of that line. We don’t need MoP completionism to understand 2026; we need the emotional permission that **missing a whole continent is normal**, and that our characters can carry later scars (especially Legion) without having farmed every MoP daily.
