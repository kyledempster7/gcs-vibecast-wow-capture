## Cold-open line

> “Warlords says: what if we time-travel to Draenor *before* it became Outland, fight the orc clans at full power, and live in a fortress that builds itself? For a lot of people it was the most exciting trailer and the expansion where the sub quietly expired.”

---

## Talking bullets (5–8)

1. **Tone (public high-level).** Muscular, cinematic, “war movie.” Alternate-timeline Draenor; Iron Horde tech-and-clan fantasy. It *feels* closer to classic orc myth than Pandaria’s discovery vibe.

2. **Story spine in plain language (public high-level).** Garrosh (post-MoP) helps rewrite history; you fight warlords who are legends, not yet fel-corrupted into the Outland you remember from Burning Crusade. Familiar names, wrong era — that cognitive dissonance *is* the expansion.

3. **Wrath brain friction.** Wrath had a coherent “push Northrend, kill Arthas.” WoD sells a big war… then much of the day-to-day becomes **garrison management** and zone hubs. The gap between trailer and Tuesday night is the core feeling to name (without a rant).

4. **Systems that stuck (public high-level).**  
   - **Garrisons / player housing-as-hub** DNA (even if later housing is different — vault now has undead housing wishlist energy; the *desire* for a home base is old).  
   - **Follower / mission-table** culture as a design ancestor.  
   - **Ship / naval missions** late-exp flavor (name only).  
   - Raid encounters people still call gorgeous (Highmaul / Blackrock Foundry / Hellfire Citadel as *names*, not guides).

5. **“Why people left” — talking points without snark walls.**  
   - **Content cadence:** long droughts between major drops (public consensus talking point; not a spreadsheet).  
   - **Garrison success became isolation:** your base was so good you stopped needing the world.  
   - **Flying / exploration friction:** gated or late for many (feeling of “the world is beautiful and I’m stuck on the ground longer than I wanted”).  
   - **Story promise vs patch reality:** Iron Horde hype → many felt the middle sagged.  
   - **Social bleed:** friends unsub at different times; the “empty garrison” became a meme for loneliness, not just design.  
   **Tone rule:** “this is what the room felt like,” not “idiots who hated garrisons.”

6. **What stuck for modern chat (public high-level).** People still say “I quit in WoD” as a **calendar stamp**. Also: some of the best raid *spectacle* of the era. Both can be true in one sentence.

7. **Character bridge (vault).** No dedicated WoD campaign evidence in vault. Honest track: **we may have barely touched this.** Contrast with Legion, where Pain’s **Deathlord** and Havoc’s **DH** class *are* identity. WoD is the gap we can admit without shame.

8. **Takeaway line.** WoD is the expansion that teaches returnees: **missing content is not a moral failure** — sometimes the game’s own structure made it easy to walk away, and the story still continues without you.

## Bridge to Midnight

WoD’s scar on the player base is **permission to leave**. That matters in 2026: a Midnight return is allowed to be *selective*. We do not owe the garrison years a full clear. The lore line from WoD into Legion is roughly “this war cracks open the bigger Burning Legion problem” (**public high-level**). Our characters pick up the *next* chapter harder than this one — Deathlord, Demon Hunter, modern duo nights. Midnight sits after even more skipped or skimmed years; the lesson from WoD is: **name the gap, keep the friendship, play the chapter that has heat.**
